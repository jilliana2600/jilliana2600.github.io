Professional Projects

Safety Testing Fixture - Banner Engineering

As an Electrical Engineering Intern at Banner Engineering, I redesigned and rebuilt a legacy safety test fixture based on 20-year-old schematics and previous testing equipment, replacing outdated components and improving the overall functionality and reliability of the fixture. I worked closely with the Safety Testing Team, as well as electrical and mechanical engineers, to ensure that all aspect of the fixture met standards and performed reliably under all testing conditions. New functionality was added to improve the fixture's capabilities and user experience. I redid old schematics, created a new PCB layout, ensured the housing and mechanical components were properly integrated, and thoroughly tested the fixture to ensure it worked as expected. As expected, there were a few issues that needed to be addressed, and I worked to identify and resolve them.

Personal Projects

Bright Electronics Manufacturing Challenge - Line Following Car

I am part of a three-person team competing in the 2026 Bright Electronics Manufacturing Challenge, where university teams design and build a custom PCB to control a line-following robot. I worked primarily on an added navigation feature that uses an IMU and dual optical wheel encoders to provide additional information about the robot’s movement. I designed the schematic and worked on the PCB layout and routing for this feature, as well as completed documentation on the added feature and PCB layout choices.
Our team placed third overall in the PCB and schematic design round out of 31 university teams from across the country. We are now preparing for the final rounds of the competition, which will take place in a few weeks at SMTA International in Rosemont, Illinois. The final competition will give us the opportunity to fabricate, test, and compete with our design in person against the other finalist teams.

Logic and Digital Design - Line Following Robo-Car

As a final project for Logic and Digital Design, I designed and built a line following Robo-Car using physical logic gates, IR obstacle detection sensors, line detection sensors, and a motor driver. The car was designed to pass beginner, intermediate, and advanced tracks, with iterative improvements such as additional sensors, PWM speed control, and reverse motor logic to optimize performance.

Circuit Modeling 2 - Transient Analysis of RLC Circuit

Perfboard RLC circuit used for transient analysis lab.

As a final lab project for Circuit Modeling 2, I performed a transient analysis of my own RLC Circuit. I was given a desired resonant frequency for the circuit and had to calculate the necessary resistor, inductor, and capacitor values to achieve this frequency. The circuit was then built on a perfboard and tested using a function generator and oscilloscope to observe the transient response and ensure that the response of the circuit matched theoretical predictions.
